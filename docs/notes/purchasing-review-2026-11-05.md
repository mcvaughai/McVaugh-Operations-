## 4. Operational facts worth keeping

**Lumber is the one genuinely time-critical PO.** Construction starts at 08:00 and a lumber PO cannot wait
until 10 or 11, or the same-day truck is missed. BFS will not jump the queue: "there's 93 lumber packages
in front of you, you'll get it when you get it if you don't get me the PO on time." The reason shortages
appear with no warning: a lumber package is about $37,000, stacked on pallets, and nobody counts studs, so
a missing beam only surfaces when the framer reaches for it. **Only lumber is this urgent.**

**Lowe's, Amazon and Home Depot POs need no approval.** The material is already bought; just enter the PO.
Everything else that arrives as a bid goes to Lonnie first — "is this good to go, can I enter this?"

**Subcontractor versus vendor.** A subcontractor supplies labour on our sites, so needs insurance, the
subcontractor agreement and the full packet. A vendor supplies material only, like the lumber company, and
needs contact details and a W-9 — a different packet, in Public.

**Dependable Builders is changing its name.** A new subcontractor agreement has arrived. A work order
already raised for them, not yet started, has to be replaced under the new name. Instructions are in
Maria's Teams message.

**The Landing.** Frank's team runs on BuilderTrend. MCH does not enter labour or material work orders or
POs there. MCH writes **one work order to Frank Martin Development with seven draws** — he is MCH's
subcontractor on that project. Their "change orders" are what they call upgrades, such as wood floor
instead of carpet, and are not purchasing's or construction's concern.
