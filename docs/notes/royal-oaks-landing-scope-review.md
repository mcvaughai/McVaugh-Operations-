## 1. Settle this before signing — it is worth $98,700

Section Four contradicts itself three ways over.

- The section is headed **REMAINING ALLOWANCES**.
- Its opening sentence: *"These ten items have no fixed specification yet and are carried as allowances until quotations are obtained."*
- Its very next sentence: *"Categories highlighted below have been discussed and agreed as STANDARD, not allowances"* — with no full stop, reading like a later insertion.
- **Every row in the table beneath is highlighted.** Taken literally, nothing is left as an allowance.
- The signature page nonetheless states **TOTAL ALLOWANCES CARRIED $98,700**.

Those cannot all be true, and which one holds decides who pays. The document's own terms:

> Where the actual cost exceeds the allowance, the difference plus standard overhead and profit is due by Change Order before the item is ordered.

If the eleven categories are allowances, McVaugh carries the overages plus Frank's overhead and
profit. If they are standard, they sit inside the contract price. **Ask Frank to state which, in
writing, before anyone signs.**

## 2. What McVaugh carries — the "(by owner)" items

Three are unambiguous:

1. Geotechnical investigation and soil report
2. Form survey and final survey (Frank also excludes title insurance, appraisal, and all lender and closing costs)
3. Water, sewer and wastewater impact fees

The fourth is not. The site and permitting line reads:

> Survey and form-board survey, foundation check, HOA and ARC submittal, **(by owner)** excavation,
> select fill, temporary power, water and sanitary facilities, silt fence and stormwater controls,
> dumpster and site cleanup.

The marker sits mid-sentence. Whether it governs what comes before it or everything after decides
whether McVaugh pays for excavation, select fill, temporary power and water, silt fence, stormwater
controls, dumpster and site cleanup. Ask Frank to re-punctuate the line.
